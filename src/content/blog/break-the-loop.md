# Break the Loop

May 17, 2026

Today is Sunday, May 17, and I'm preparing for the work week.

I'm also sitting inside one of the most honest realizations I've had since I started building with AI:

I have been building constantly, but I have not been shipping enough.

I finally committed to the Claude Max plan. I'm using Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT, Obsidian, GitHub, and all these new AI-native workflows. I've vibe-coded my website. I've built frameworks, systems, prompts, protocols, repo structures, agent concepts, automation ideas.

And on paper, a lot of it looked impressive.

But outside of my website, not enough had actually gone to market.

That forced me to ask a harder question:

Why was I still stuck?

The answer was uncomfortable because it had nothing to do with intelligence.

The problem was not lack of capability.
The problem was not lack of ideas.
The problem was not even lack of effort.

The problem was the loop.

Every morning I would wake up and check what happened overnight in AI.

A new Claude feature.
A new coding workflow.
A new memory system.
A new agent framework.
A new YouTube video explaining a "better" way to build.

And because all of it felt relevant, I immediately tried to integrate it.

That was the loop.

I would tell myself:

> "This will make the system better."

Then I would:

- open another project folder
- redesign the architecture
- rebuild the workflow
- rewrite the prompts
- restructure the framework
- ask for another sanity check

Then the day would end.

And the next morning I would ask the same question again:

Where am I in the build?

That became the signal.

Not the tools.
Not the frameworks.
Not the AI.

The signal was the fact that I kept losing my footing.

And that realization was humbling because the very thing I'm trying to help founders solve is the exact thing I was struggling with myself.

I talk about operational clarity.
I talk about systems.
I talk about signal over noise.
I talk about helping founders move from chaos into deployment.

But I had to admit something:

I was not fully practicing what I preach.

I had frameworks without containment.
I had ideas without closure.
I had intelligence without operational discipline.

## What Claude Saw

Then Claude called me out.

I had developed this lazy workflow where I would take outputs from ChatGPT and throw them into Claude, then take Claude outputs and move them back into ChatGPT. I assumed that because the models had context, I was making progress.

But I was not actually slowing down enough to convert information into decisions.

I was accumulating insight without operationalizing it.

And during one of those sessions, Claude stopped me in real time.

It literally said:

> "You did it again."

That sentence hit harder than I expected.

Because it was right.

I would have a breakthrough about my behavior and instantly use that breakthrough as justification to start another overhaul.

Another rebuild.
Another redesign.
Another "better" version of the system.

Even my self-awareness had become part of the loop.

Later, while researching the behavior itself, I found a term for part of what I was experiencing:

**Procrastivity.**

A form of productive procrastination where you stay busy doing useful-feeling work while still avoiding the highest-priority task.

That explained a lot.

Because I was never doing "nothing."

I was constantly:

- researching
- building
- documenting
- refining
- prompting
- architecting
- optimizing

The work itself was real.

But the market outcome never fully materialized because I kept resetting the process before completion.

And if you're neurodivergent or ADHD like me, the problem gets amplified because novelty feels energizing.

A new AI feature feels exciting.
A new workflow feels important.
A new framework feels like the missing piece.

Research around ADHD and novelty-seeking behavior shows the brain constantly searches for stimulation and activation. Which means new information creates a real motivational spike.

Suddenly, my behavior made sense.

Not as laziness.

As a loop.

A loop where building became safer than shipping.

Because once something ships, the market gets a vote.

Once I saw that clearly, I finally understood the structure of the loop:

New information.
New excitement.
New implementation.
New project folder.
New uncertainty.
New sanity check.
No deployment.

And the scariest part?

The entire time, it feels productive.

That's why I think so many builders are trapped inside this right now, especially in AI.

Because AI dramatically lowered the barrier to creation.

But it also lowered the friction to endless ideation.

You can now build forever without ever confronting the market.

## How I Started Breaking the Loop

What finally changed was simple.

I stopped treating every new realization like a reason to rebuild everything.

That was the pattern.

Every insight became:

- another overhaul
- another framework
- another architecture redesign
- another "better" version of the system

I was using new information as justification to restart instead of complete.

So I forced myself to constrain the scope.

Instead of asking:

> "What should I build next?"

I started asking:

> "What already exists that is closest to deployment?"

That question changed everything.

Because when I audited the repo honestly, I realized I was not years away from shipping.

I was a handful of PRs away.

My website already existed.
The infrastructure already existed.
The messaging already existed.

It was incomplete, but it was real.

And psychologically, that distinction mattered.

I was not starting from zero.

I was avoiding closure.

Once I understood that, I changed the rules.

No opening new project folders.
No rebuilding the architecture.
No integrating every new AI workflow I discovered online.

Just:

- finish the existing work
- close the open loops
- ship what already exists
- let reality respond

That was the break.

Not some massive transformation.

A containment decision.

I stopped treating ideation as progress.

And I started treating completion as the metric.

That shift changed the way I think about AI entirely.

Because AI makes starting incredibly easy.

But if you are not careful, it creates an environment where you can endlessly create without ever confronting whether the thing actually works.

And ironically, through all of this, I realized something even bigger:

My own experience was the missing messaging on my website.

Not just the frameworks.
Not just the terminology.
Not just the systems.

The honesty.

The recognition that a lot of intelligent builders are quietly trapped inside recursive optimization loops while convincing themselves they are making progress.

Because I stopped trying to sound like someone who had all the answers.

And started writing from a place that was operationally true.

Not:

> "I mastered the system."

But:

> "I found the bottleneck."

And the bottleneck was not capability.

It was unfinished closure loops disguised as productivity.

If you've opened three new project folders this week and finished none of them, you are probably not behind on work.

You are probably in the loop.

And sometimes the only way forward is to stop reinventing the system long enough to finish the version already sitting in front of you.

That is how I started breaking mine.
