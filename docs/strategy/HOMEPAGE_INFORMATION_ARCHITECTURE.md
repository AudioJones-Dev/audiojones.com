## 1. Role Of This Artifact

The homepage information architecture is the reference implementation pattern for future public artifacts.

It should prove that the Constitution can become a founder-facing page without turning into internal doctrine, generic AI positioning, or disconnected marketing copy.

## 2. M2 Definition Of Done

The homepage is complete only if it satisfies all fidelity checks below.

| Fidelity Check | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutional Fidelity | Implements the constitutional principles, not merely references them. |
| Doctrinal Fidelity | Accurately translates doctrine without leaking internal language. |
| Strategic Fidelity | Establishes Founder Intelligence as the category, ResponseOS as proof, and Founder Intelligence Diagnostic as the strategic engagement. |
| Translation Fidelity | A founder understands the page without needing AJ Digital's internal architecture. |
| Commercial Fidelity | The visitor understands the problem, category, path, and next action. |
| Evidence Fidelity | Every major claim can trace to Business Memory, client evidence, research, or doctrine. |

## 3. Page Constitution

Every homepage section must answer these six questions before copy is written.

1. Why does this section exist?
2. What constitutional principle does it express?
3. What doctrine is being translated?
4. What strategic decision does it support?
5. What evidence justifies its claims?
6. What single decision should the visitor be able to make after reading it?

## 5. Homepage Job

The homepage should make a first-time founder recognize that their business problem is not primarily an AI, automation, or lead-volume problem.

It should help them understand that the business has a signal, memory, judgment, or execution problem that should be diagnosed before anything is built.

The homepage should not sell every offer. It should create diagnostic intent.

## 6. Required Belief Shift

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| I need AI. | I need to understand what is actually happening before I add AI. |
| I need automation. | I need a system that improves judgment and execution. |
| I need more leads. | I may be leaking revenue from the leads I already have. |
| I need a tool. | I need diagnosis, then design, then implementation. |
| My business is busy but unclear. | My business has a signal problem. |

## 7. Core Message Stack

The homepage should express three public messages in order.

1. Problem message: the visitor has a signal problem, not merely a growth problem.
2. Thesis message: the business already contains answers, but lacks a reliable way to observe, preserve, and act on them.
3. Category message: Founder Intelligence helps founder-led businesses see what is actually happening, make better decisions, and execute consistently. AI is one tool used to make that possible.

These are message functions, not final copy.

## 8. Information Hierarchy

### Priority 1 - Immediate Recognition

The visitor should quickly recognize the pain: unclear signals, missed follow-up, scattered knowledge, founder bottlenecks, and weak decision support.

### Priority 2 - Category Naming

The page should name Founder Intelligence as the category after the problem is recognized.

### Priority 3 - Method

The page should make the method clear: diagnose before building.

### Priority 4 - Proof

The page should show ResponseOS as the clearest proof that the philosophy can become a measurable system.

### Priority 5 - Capability Expansion

The page should introduce supporting capabilities such as Business Memory, Revenue Intelligence, AI Receptionist, and Operating Blueprint only after the category and method are clear.

### Priority 6 - Conversion

The page should route the visitor toward the Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.

## 9. Recommended Section Order

1. Hero: problem recognition and category direction
2. Problem framing: what the signal problem looks like
3. Founder Intelligence: category explanation
4. Method: diagnose before building
5. Proof: ResponseOS as applied Founder Intelligence
6. Capabilities: Business Memory, Revenue Intelligence, AI Receptionist, Operating Blueprint
7. Commercial path: diagnostic to blueprint to system
8. Trust: Audio Jones as translator, AJ Digital as operating company
9. Final CTA: Founder Intelligence Diagnostic
10. Footer narrative: category coherence and navigation continuation

## 10. Narrative Flow

The scroll should follow this psychological sequence.

| Stage | Visitor State | Page Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | "This sounds like my business." | Name the signal problem in founder language. |
| Reframe | "Maybe my issue is not more AI or more leads." | Explain that reality, memory, judgment, and execution must come before automation. |
| Category | "What is this called?" | Introduce Founder Intelligence. |
| Method | "How would this work?" | Show diagnosis before design and implementation. |
| Proof | "Is this real?" | Use ResponseOS as a concrete proof product. |
| Scope | "What else does this cover?" | Show supporting capabilities without turning the page into a catalog. |
| Action | "What should I do next?" | Route to Founder Intelligence Diagnostic. |

## 11. Section-Level Architecture

### 1. Hero

Purpose:
Create immediate recognition and establish the category direction.

Information to include:
- Signal problem.
- Founder-led business context.
- Founder Intelligence as category direction.
- Primary CTA to Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.

Information to exclude:
- Product list.
- AI-first language.
- Internal constitution or doctrine language.
- Unsupported metrics.

Primary visitor question:
"Is this person describing what is actually happening inside my business?"

Primary belief:
"This understands my operating problem."

CTA placement:
Primary CTA visible above the fold. Secondary link may point to the method, not to a competing offer.

### 2. Problem Framing

Purpose:
Translate "signal problem" into concrete symptoms.

Information to include:
- Missed follow-up.
- Unclear attribution.
- Scattered decisions and knowledge.
- Founder-dependent execution.
- Busy activity without confidence.

Information to exclude:
- Abstract organizational cognition language.
- More than one new term at a time.
- Long problem taxonomy.

Primary visitor question:
"What does a signal problem look like?"

Primary belief:
"This is not only a marketing or lead problem."

CTA placement:
No hard CTA required. Move the visitor into the category explanation.

### 3. Founder Intelligence Category

Purpose:
Name and define the external category.

Information to include:
- Seeing what is actually happening.
- Preserving what matters.
- Making better decisions.
- Executing consistently.
- AI as one leverage tool after the category is understood.

Information to exclude:
- Founder Intelligence as software-only.
- Founder Intelligence as vague advisory.
- AI as the category.

Primary visitor question:
"What is Founder Intelligence?"

Primary belief:
"This is a disciplined way to improve how the business thinks and acts."

CTA placement:
Soft CTA to diagnostic path or method section.

### 4. Method: Diagnose Before Building

Purpose:
Show the operating sequence that governs the engagement.

Information to include:
- Observe.
- Diagnose.
- Design.
- Implement.
- Measure.
- The Founder Intelligence Diagnostic as the first strategic engagement.

Information to exclude:
- Full process detail.
- Delivery operations.
- Internal ADL/COS terminology.

Primary visitor question:
"How do I start without buying the wrong thing?"

Primary belief:
"The diagnostic is the right next step because implementation without diagnosis is risky."

CTA placement:
Primary CTA can repeat after the method section.

### 5. Proof: ResponseOS

Purpose:
Make the philosophy tangible through a concrete product wedge.

Information to include:
- Revenue leak.
- Follow-up recovery.
- Capture, qualify, route, recover as functional proof.
- ResponseOS as applied Founder Intelligence.

Information to exclude:
- Chatbot framing.
- ResponseOS as the whole company.
- Unverified performance claims.
- Full pricing table.

Primary visitor question:
"What does Founder Intelligence look like when implemented?"

Primary belief:
"This can become a real operating system, not just a philosophy."

CTA placement:
Product CTA may appear here, but must remain secondary to Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.

### 6. Capabilities

Purpose:
Show the broader system without creating a product catalog.

Information to include:
- Business Memory.
- Revenue Intelligence.
- AI Receptionist.
- Operating Blueprint.
- Each capability as a response to a business problem.

Information to exclude:
- Full suite naming sprawl.
- Premature product labels.
- Multi-agent systems language.
- Internal knowledge architecture.

Primary visitor question:
"What kinds of business problems can this address?"

Primary belief:
"The work covers the operating system, not just one tool."

CTA placement:
No competing CTA. Keep the diagnostic path as the next move.

### 7. Commercial Path

Purpose:
Clarify what happens after interest.

Information to include:
- Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.
- Operating Blueprint.
- Founder Intelligence System.
- Ongoing operating partnership only if named as provisional or kept generic.

Information to exclude:
- Full pricing table.
- Too many entry points.
- Unratified ongoing-offer name as final.

Primary visitor question:
"What happens after I raise my hand?"

Primary belief:
"There is a disciplined path from diagnosis to implementation."

CTA placement:
Primary CTA should be repeated at the end of this section.

### 8. Trust / About

Purpose:
Make the entity relationship understandable without overexplaining it.

Information to include:
- Audio Jones as public translator and operator.
- AJ Digital as the company behind systems, research, and implementation.
- Founder-facing credibility.

Information to exclude:
- Long biography.
- Legacy creative, podcast, studio, or generic consulting identity.
- Internal entity constitution.

Primary visitor question:
"Why should I trust this person and company?"

Primary belief:
"Audio Jones can explain the problem clearly and AJ Digital can operationalize the solution."

CTA placement:
Diagnostic CTA or About link. Avoid making About the main action.

### 9. Final CTA

Purpose:
Convert recognition into diagnostic intent.

Information to include:
- One primary next step: Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.
- One secondary path at most.
- Short expectation of what the diagnostic clarifies.

Information to exclude:
- Multiple equal CTAs.
- Book-a-call language if the action is not direct scheduling.
- Scorecard, calculator, apply, and product CTAs all competing.

Primary visitor question:
"What should I do now?"

Primary belief:
"I should start with diagnosis."

CTA placement:
Final section, high contrast, single dominant action.

### 10. Footer Narrative

Purpose:
Reinforce the category and keep the site coherent after the main story.

Information to include:
- Founder Intelligence.
- Diagnostic path.
- Insights / frameworks.
- Legal links.

Information to exclude:
- Legacy studio language.
- Generic creative agency positioning.
- Broad AI consulting language.

Primary visitor question:
"Where do I go if I am not ready to convert?"

Primary belief:
"The whole site is built around the same operating thesis."

CTA placement:
Navigation continuation, not a second homepage argument.

## 12. CTA Hierarchy

### Primary CTA

Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.

### Secondary CTA

See the diagnostic path or see how it works.

### Tertiary CTA

Explore ResponseOS, only inside the proof section.

### Acquisition Mechanism

Signal Scorecard may be used as a lower-friction entry, but it should not replace the homepage's strategic CTA unless the funnel strategy changes.

### CTA Rules

- One primary CTA at a time.
- Product CTAs must not compete with the diagnostic CTA.
- Book-a-call language should only be used if the user can actually book a call.
- Calculator and scorecard CTAs should support the diagnostic path, not fragment it.

## 13. Proof Placement

Proof should appear after the category and method are established.

Recommended proof order:

1. ResponseOS as concrete implementation proof.
2. Revenue leak framing as the business problem ResponseOS addresses.
3. Any measurable claims only if evidence is available and traceable.
4. Placeholder or representative metrics should be avoided or clearly labeled.

Proof should answer:

- Has this philosophy produced a real system?
- Can the system create measurable business outcomes?
- Is the evidence specific enough to trust?

## 14. Objection Handling

| Visitor Objection | Where It Should Be Handled | Response Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Is this just AI automation?" | Hero, category, method | AI is the leverage layer, not the category. |
| "Do I need another tool?" | Problem framing, method | The first step is diagnosis, not buying software. |
| "Can this help my actual business?" | Problem framing, capabilities | Map symptoms to business operating problems. |
| "Is this a chatbot?" | ResponseOS proof | ResponseOS is revenue recovery infrastructure, not a chatbot. |
| "What happens after I start?" | Commercial path | Diagnostic leads to blueprint, system, and ongoing support. |
| "Why should I trust Audio?" | Trust / about | Audio Jones translates; AJ Digital operationalizes. |
| "Is this too abstract?" | All sections | Use founder language before doctrine terms. |

## 15. Scroll Psychology

### First 5 Seconds

Visitor should feel recognized.

Page task:
Make the signal problem legible.

### First 30 Seconds

Visitor should understand:

- The problem.
- The category.
- The next action.

Page task:
Move from recognition to diagnostic intent.

### Mid-Page

Visitor should understand:

- Why AI is not the starting point.
- How Founder Intelligence works.
- Why ResponseOS is proof.

Page task:
Build confidence without overexplaining.

### End Of Page

Visitor should understand:

- What to do next.
- Why the diagnostic is the correct next move.
- Where to go if not ready.

Page task:
Convert clarity into action or continued trust.

## 16. Claim And Evidence Rules

- Do not publish performance metrics unless the evidence source is identified.
- Use modeled estimates only if labeled as modeled.
- Do not use generic AI market stats as proof.
- Do not use legacy studio or creative-agency proof as Founder Intelligence proof.
- If a claim cannot trace to research, Business Memory, client evidence, or doctrine, treat it as provisional.

## 17. Risks

- The page becomes too abstract for service-business founders.
- Internal doctrine leaks into public copy.
- The primary CTA competes with scorecard, calculator, product, and booking CTAs.
- Founder Intelligence sounds like software only.
- AI disappears so much that buyers do not understand implementation capability.
- ResponseOS appears to be the entire company.
- Business Memory appears too early and dilutes the first impression.
- The homepage becomes a product catalog instead of a category introduction.

## 18. Copywriting Readiness Checklist

Copywriting may begin only when these are true:

- Primary CTA remains Founder Intelligence Diagnostic.
- Scorecard role is defined as acquisition mechanism.
- Business Memory is treated as supporting capability.
- ResponseOS is treated as proof product.
- AI is treated as leverage layer.
- Section order is approved.
- Claims requiring evidence are identified.
- No unresolved strategic naming issue blocks the page.

## 19. RI-001 Review Gate

RI-001 should be reviewed as a constitutional implementation, not as a normal website page.

### Gate 1 - Constitutional Fidelity

Question:
Does every section exist because the Constitution requires it, not because websites usually have that section?

Failure condition:
A section exists only by convention and cannot cite a constitutional objective.

### Gate 2 - Narrative Fidelity

Question:
Does the visitor naturally move through this progression?

```txt
Signal Problem
↓
Founder Intelligence
↓
Diagnosis
↓
ResponseOS
↓
Capabilities
↓
Diagnostic CTA
```

Failure condition:
The page jumps into products, proof, or CTAs before the problem and category are understood.

### Gate 3 - Category Fidelity

Question:
Could a visitor accidentally conclude that Audio Jones is primarily an AI consulting or automation company?

Failure condition:
AI becomes the category instead of Founder Intelligence.

### Gate 4 - Translation Fidelity

Question:
Does every internal term have a founder-language translation?

Required translations:

| Internal | Public |
| --- | --- |
| Operational Reality | What's actually happening inside your business |
| Business Memory | A system that remembers how your business works |
| Organizational Cognition | Never public |
| Founder Intelligence | Decision support for founder-led businesses |

Failure condition:
Internal doctrine language appears without translation.

### Gate 5 - Commercial Fidelity

Question:
Can the visitor answer these within one scroll?

- What problem do they solve?
- Why is it different?
- Why should I trust them?
- What happens next?

Failure condition:
The page creates interest but not commercial clarity.

### Gate 6 - Evidence Fidelity

Question:
Can every major claim answer: where did we learn this?

Valid sources:

- Business Memory.
- Research.
- Client evidence.
- Doctrine.
- Observed operational reality.

Failure condition:
The page relies on unsupported claims, generic AI market statistics, or legacy proof.

### Gate 7 - Simplicity Test

Question:
Could Jon Taffer explain this to a plumbing contractor?

Failure condition:
The concept requires internal architecture knowledge to understand.
