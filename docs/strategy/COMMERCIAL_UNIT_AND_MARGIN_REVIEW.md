## 1. Executive recommendation

AJ Digital should not translate the canonical offer ladder into a single
software-style pricing table. The work contains four different economic
models:

1. **Diagnose:** fixed-fee, tightly bounded professional analysis.
2. **Architect:** fixed-fee, scope-bounded knowledge and operating-system
   design.
3. **Install:** one-time implementation priced from a base unit plus approved
   modules, complexity, and risk.
4. **Operate:** monthly managed service with explicit capacity, support, and
   usage boundaries.

The recommended commercial architecture is:

```text
Free assessment
  → fixed-fee Founder Intelligence Diagnostic
  → fixed-fee Business Memory and SOP Architecture
  → one-time System Installation
      + separately metered or passed-through variable usage
  → monthly Managed Intelligence
      + separately approved change orders for net-new installation scope
```

The current public numbers are not supported by a repository-visible cost
model. They should therefore be treated as **commercially unverified legacy
prices**, not as evidence that the canonical offers can be delivered at the
required margin.

No new price point should be ratified until AJ Digital has:

- defined each sellable unit and its exclusions;
- costed founder and contractor delivery labor at replacement cost;
- separated installation, recurring service, and variable vendor usage;
- included quality assurance, rework, support, and risk reserves;
- calculated both gross margin and contribution margin;
- tested the resulting price against buyer value and willingness to pay.

## 5. Commercial-unit doctrine

### 5.1 Definition

A **commercial unit** is the smallest independently sellable scope that AJ
Digital can:

- describe without ambiguity;
- estimate from observable work;
- deliver to a stated acceptance condition;
- support within a defined boundary;
- cost without treating founder labor as free;
- expand through named add-on units or a change order.

The client buys an outcome and bounded deliverables. AJ Digital should still
measure the internal labor, vendor, support, and risk cost required to deliver
that outcome.

### 5.2 Rules for every unit

Every order form or statement of work should state:

1. client entity, brand, location, and operating domain in scope;
2. primary business outcome;
3. included stakeholders, workflows, channels, systems, sources, and
   integrations;
4. deliverables and acceptance criteria;
5. client dependencies and required access;
6. review and revision limits;
7. estimated calendar window and client-delay policy;
8. stabilization or warranty window;
9. support hours and response posture;
10. variable usage and third-party cost treatment;
11. exclusions and change-order triggers;
12. data, governance, retention, and authority boundaries.

### 5.3 Recommended economic types

| Canonical stage | Primary economic type | Revenue unit | Expansion mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assess | Acquisition | Completed assessment or qualified lead | Marketing-budget decision |
| Diagnose | Fixed professional service | One bounded diagnostic | Additional operating domain or evidence pack |
| Architect | Fixed architecture service | One architecture core | Workflow/SOP blocks, source domains, governance extensions |
| Install | Fixed implementation | One base installation | Integration, channel, location, agent, workflow, or application modules |
| Operate | Recurring managed service | One managed-system capacity band per month | More systems, service level, change capacity, or usage |

### 5.4 Non-negotiable commercial separations

- Discovery and architecture are not free presales for an installation.
- Installation is not hidden inside a low monthly fee.
- Monthly management does not include unlimited net-new build work.
- Variable third-party usage is not silently absorbed.
- Client-caused delay and source-data remediation are not unlimited.
- A multi-module Founder Intelligence System is not priced as a discounted
  bundle by default.
- Performance compensation does not replace a cost-covering base fee.

## 6. Cost and margin method

### 6.1 Net collected revenue

For internal offer analysis:

```text
Net collected revenue
= contracted service fee
- discounts
- refunds and credits
- payment-processing cost when AJ Digital absorbs it
- uncollectible amounts
```

Taxes collected for a government authority and client-funded pass-through
costs are excluded from service revenue for margin analysis.

### 6.2 Delivery cost of goods sold

Delivery COGS includes costs caused directly by serving the client:

- founder delivery labor at a documented replacement cost;
- employee delivery labor with payroll burden and benefits;
- contractor and specialist labor;
- delivery project management;
- client-specific discovery and architecture;
- implementation, integration, migration, and configuration;
- quality assurance, security review, and production acceptance;
- training and handoff;
- included revisions and rework;
- included stabilization and support;
- client-specific software and infrastructure;
- absorbed model, telephony, messaging, storage, and automation usage;
- travel or other direct fulfillment expense;
- a scope-risk reserve appropriate to uncertainty.

General brand marketing, unrelated product development, general
administration, and owner profit distribution are not delivery COGS. They
belong below gross margin when calculating contribution and operating profit.

### 6.3 Founder labor rule

Founder delivery labor must never be costed at zero.

Use the higher of:

- the fully loaded cost of a qualified replacement;
- the founder's approved internal delivery rate derived from target
  compensation and realistic productive hours.

Do not divide annual compensation by all calendar work hours. Remove sales,
administration, professional development, holidays, leave, and realistic
non-billable capacity before calculating the productive-hour denominator.

### 6.4 Margin formulas

```text
Gross profit = net collected revenue - delivery COGS

Gross margin % = gross profit / net collected revenue

Contribution profit =
  gross profit
  - directly attributable sales commission
  - offer-specific acquisition cost
  - offer-specific non-delivery operating expense

Contribution margin % =
  contribution profit / net collected revenue
```

The minimum viable service fee is:

```text
Minimum fee at margin floor =
  risk-adjusted delivery COGS / (1 - approved gross-margin floor)
```

The target service fee is:

```text
Target fee =
  risk-adjusted delivery COGS / (1 - approved target gross margin)
```

Value, urgency, switching cost, and buyer willingness to pay may justify a
higher price. They do not justify pricing below the risk-adjusted cost floor.

### 6.5 Cost confidence

Each offer should carry two internal cost estimates:

- **P50 cost:** expected delivery under normal conditions.
- **P80 cost:** cost that should not be exceeded in approximately four out of
  five comparable deliveries.

Use P80—not the optimistic estimate—to test the minimum margin floor until
there is enough observed delivery data to use a more reliable forecast.

## 7. Provisional internal margin policy

These are founder-ratified, provisional internal planning bands. They are not
accounting advice, contract terms, dollar prices, or public claims. They must be
recalibrated from observed delivery evidence.

| Offer | Target gross margin | Approval floor | Why |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Founder Intelligence Diagnostic | 65–75% | 55% | Bounded intellectual work should fund expertise, sales time, and downstream prescription |
| Business Memory and SOP Architecture | 60–70% | 50% | Source quality and stakeholder synthesis create moderate delivery variance |
| ResponseOS Installation | 50–60% | 45% | Integration, QA, vendor behavior, and stabilization add execution risk |
| AI Agent System Installation | 50–60% | 45% | Tool, source, evaluation, and approval complexity can create rework |
| Custom Application Build | 45–55% | 40% | Bespoke software has the highest estimation and defect risk |
| Founder Intelligence System Installation | 50–60% | 45% | Program coordination and cross-module dependency must be funded |
| Managed Intelligence | 60–70% steady state | 50% | Recurring work can standardize, but support and change creep must be bounded |

Policy recommendations:

- Quote to the target range, not the floor.
- The floor is an exception boundary, not a sales target.
- Any quote below the floor requires a written reason, named approving owner,
  and a strategic benefit measurable enough to audit.
- Discounts reduce margin; they do not reduce delivery cost. Every discount
  requires a matching scope reduction, term commitment, prepayment benefit, or
  documented acquisition rationale.
- Recalculate the margin after delivery using actual labor, vendor, support,
  rework, and collection data.
- Review targets after the first three comparable deliveries and quarterly
  thereafter.

## 8. Founder-ratified offer units

### 8.1 Founder Intelligence Diagnostic

**Recommended base unit**

One bounded diagnostic for one operating entity and one primary revenue or
execution journey, producing the canonical decision-ready findings and one
recommended next-system path.

**Proposed initial scope cap**

- one operating entity;
- one primary customer or operating journey;
- up to four stakeholder interviews;
- up to six approved evidence sources or systems;
- one current-state synthesis;
- one decision readout;
- one consolidated factual-correction cycle;
- one final decision-ready report.

These caps are an initial pilot hypothesis. They must be revised from actual
delivery evidence.

**Add-on or change units**

- additional operating entity;
- additional journey or department;
- additional stakeholder block;
- additional evidence-source block;
- data cleanup or reconstruction;
- expedited delivery;
- on-site work;
- extended facilitation or workshop.

**Cost drivers**

Intake quality, stakeholder availability, evidence volume, source
accessibility, contradictory testimony, data cleanup, analysis depth,
facilitation, and revision load.

**Acceptance condition**

The client receives the defined operating map, fragmentation and leak
findings, risk assessment, prioritized recommendations, and one prescribed
architecture or installation path. Implementation is excluded.

### 8.2 Business Memory and SOP Architecture

**Recommended base unit**

One architecture core for one operating domain, containing:

- source and authority map;
- canonical terminology;
- retention, exclusion, confidence, and escalation rules;
- business-memory architecture;
- three priority workflow/SOP designs;
- human and agent instruction patterns;
- maintenance and change-control plan.

**Expansion units**

- additional three-SOP workflow block;
- additional business domain;
- additional source-system assessment;
- regulated or restricted-data policy extension;
- additional agent-context package;
- implementation-ready data mapping;
- facilitated validation session.

**Cost drivers**

Undocumented exceptions, conflicting sources, sensitive-data categories,
number of roles, workflow variance, source quality, approval latency, and the
amount of reconstruction required.

**Acceptance condition**

The architecture is internally consistent, source-backed, owner-approved, and
specific enough to guide a later installation. Building the memory store,
agent, automation, or application is excluded unless separately sold.

### 8.3 ResponseOS Installation

**Recommended base installation**

- one operating entity or brand;
- one location;
- one primary inbound channel or number;
- one CRM or system of record;
- one qualification and routing matrix;
- one recovery sequence;
- one escalation path;
- one reporting view;
- one governance and capture policy;
- production acceptance, training, and a finite stabilization window.

**Expansion units**

- additional location or brand;
- additional channel or telephone number;
- additional language;
- additional CRM or integration;
- additional routing or recovery workflow;
- custom attribution mapping;
- advanced reporting;
- regulated-data controls;
- migration or source-data remediation;
- non-standard service level or after-hours human coverage.

**Recurring unit**

One managed ResponseOS environment per month with explicitly stated:

- included interaction or usage band;
- system health review;
- workflow-performance review;
- incident and exception queue;
- reporting cadence;
- bounded tuning or optimization allowance;
- support window and response objective;
- vendor-cost treatment.

**Exclusions from the recurring base**

- new integrations;
- new locations or channels;
- major workflow redesign;
- historical data migration;
- custom application development;
- material governance-policy expansion;
- 24/7 human support;
- client-side CRM cleanup;
- third-party fees outside the included usage band.

### 8.4 AI Agent System Installation

**Recommended base unit**

One production agent role with:

- one bounded business outcome;
- one approved harness;
- one operating-instruction package;
- one authority and approval matrix;
- one primary workflow;
- a bounded set of tools and authoritative sources;
- evaluations, failure behavior, audit requirements, training, and handoff.

**Expansion units**

- additional agent role;
- additional production workflow;
- additional tool or source-system integration;
- additional business domain;
- restricted-data policy extension;
- multi-agent orchestration;
- custom evaluation suite;
- additional harness deployment;
- non-standard observability or service level.

**Acceptance condition**

The agent completes the approved workflow against the agreed evaluation set,
respects authority and escalation rules, produces the required audit evidence,
and fails safely. General autonomous capability is not an acceptance
condition.

### 8.5 Custom Application Build

**Recommended unit**

A milestone-based implementation defined by an approved architecture and
delivery specification. Do not sell a custom application from a verbal
feature list.

Required commercial boundaries:

- paid discovery or architecture completed first;
- acceptance criteria for each milestone;
- named environments and deployment responsibility;
- change-control process;
- client-data and integration dependencies;
- security and quality gates;
- warranty and maintenance boundary;
- third-party license and usage treatment.

Use a fixed fee only when the requirements and dependencies are bounded.
Otherwise use a paid discovery phase followed by milestone repricing. Do not
use an open-ended fixed fee to absorb unresolved architecture.

### 8.6 Founder Intelligence System Installation

**Recommended unit**

A governed program assembled from:

- Business Memory and SOP Architecture;
- one or more installation modules;
- integration and program-management scope;
- an acceptance plan;
- an optional Managed Intelligence transition.

Commercial rules:

- price the underlying modules first;
- add explicit program-management and cross-module QA cost;
- add dependency and risk reserves;
- do not hide custom-application work inside the umbrella label;
- do not apply a bundle discount unless delivery reuse creates a measured cost
  reduction;
- phase the engagement when evidence, access, or architecture remains
  uncertain.

### 8.7 Managed Intelligence

**Recommended recurring unit**

A monthly managed-system capacity band defined by:

- number and type of installed systems;
- review and reporting cadence;
- number of memory/SOP/evidence review cycles;
- included optimization capacity;
- incident and exception handling;
- support window and response objective;
- governance maintenance;
- variable usage and vendor-cost treatment.

Managed Intelligence includes operation, review, maintenance, and bounded
optimization. It does not include unlimited new features, new systems, major
integrations, migrations, or a continuously available founder.

The first month should not silently absorb onboarding or remediation.
Transition work is either included in the preceding installation or sold as a
separate activation unit.

### 9.2 Recurring COGS

- telephony minutes and numbers;
- SMS, email, or messaging usage;

- model inference;

- database, storage, and logging;
- monitoring and alerting;
- vendor platform minimums;
- human exception review;
- workflow tuning;
- report preparation;

### 9.3 Recommended billing structure

```text
One-time installation fee
+ monthly managed-system fee
+ included usage band
+ metered overage or pass-through vendor usage
+ separately approved change orders
```

Do not promise "unlimited" interactions, changes, workflows, integrations, or
support.

When vendor usage is passed through:

- disclose the categories and reconciliation method;
- avoid representing pass-through revenue as high-margin service revenue;
- use a separate management fee if administration and monitoring create work;
- define what happens when usage spikes or a vendor changes price.

### 9.4 Current price risk

The current $397 monthly entry point is not recommended for a
founder-supported ResponseOS installation. It could only be evaluated as a
future self-service product if setup, support, integrations, usage, and
governance work were fundamentally different from the ratified installed
system.

The $797 and $1,297 monthly tiers are also unratifiable without measured
recurring COGS, support demand, included usage, and a separately funded
installation. This is not a conclusion that the numbers are necessarily too
low for every future product. It is a conclusion that the repository provides
no evidence that they fund the canonical delivery promise.

## 10. Support, change, and risk boundaries

### 10.1 Stabilization

Each installation should include one finite stabilization window beginning at
production acceptance. The proposed default for modeling is 30 calendar days.
Its included labor must be costed into installation COGS.

Stabilization covers defects against agreed acceptance criteria. It does not
cover new requirements, changed client processes, new integrations, new data
sources, vendor product changes, or client-caused configuration changes.

### 10.2 Support

Do not sell 24/7 support until AJ Digital has staffed and costed it.

Every managed agreement should define:

- support channel;
- covered hours and time zone;
- severity definitions;
- response objective rather than an unsupported resolution guarantee;
- escalation owner;
- vendor-outage treatment;
- client-caused incident treatment;
- emergency work and after-hours rates or exclusions.

### 10.3 Change orders

A change order is required when work adds or materially changes:

- an entity, brand, location, language, channel, integration, workflow, role,
  agent, data domain, deliverable, service level, or governance category;
- source-data remediation;
- migration volume;
- acceptance criteria;
- delivery schedule caused by new client requirements;
- support or optimization capacity.

### 10.4 Client delay

Contracts should define:

- required access and decision deadlines;
- schedule movement when inputs are late;
- a pause threshold;
- remobilization cost after a prolonged pause;
- deemed acceptance or documented dispute procedures.

These are commercial recommendations and require legal review before use.

## 13. Evidence required before price ratification

### 13.1 Delivery evidence

- time by delivery phase and role;
- actual versus estimated hours;
- contractor invoices or rate cards;
- vendor setup and recurring bills;
- client-specific infrastructure costs;
- revision, rework, and defect hours;
- stabilization and ongoing support hours;
- integration failure and maintenance history;
- client-delay and remobilization history.

### 13.2 Commercial evidence

- qualified opportunities by offer;
- proposals issued;
- close and loss reasons;
- discount requests and granted discounts;
- sales-cycle duration;
- payment timing and collection loss;
- acquisition source and cost;
- expansion, renewal, contraction, and churn;
- buyer value evidence;
- revenue-recovery attribution confidence.

### 13.3 Evidence threshold

For a new offer with no historical delivery:

1. model using conservative P80 cost;
2. sell a tightly bounded pilot without public self-serve pricing;
3. record actual delivery and support effort;
4. recalculate after every delivery until three comparable engagements exist;
5. review the unit, price, and margin policy before broader publication.

Three engagements do not prove durable economics. They are the minimum
practical checkpoint for replacing pure assumption with observed delivery.

Before a proposal is sent, the owner should confirm:

- target and floor margin;
- price and discount;
- payment schedule;
- client dependencies;
- variable usage treatment;

### Post-delivery review

Within ten business days of completion:

## 15. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder labor is treated as profit instead of delivery cost | Critical | Cost founder time at replacement value before calculating margin |
| Monthly fee hides installation labor | Critical | Separate installation from managed operation |
| Variable AI or communications usage creates negative-margin clients | High | Included bands, metering, limits, and overage/pass-through policy |
| Managed Intelligence becomes unlimited founder access | High | Capacity, cadence, support, and change boundaries |
| Diagnostic expands into unpaid architecture | High | Acceptance criteria and explicit architecture exclusion |
| Architecture expands into unpaid implementation | High | Sell installation separately |
| Fixed-fee custom work absorbs unresolved requirements | High | Paid discovery, milestones, change control, and P80 reserve |
| Bundle discount removes cross-module QA funding | High | Price modules and program management before any discount |
| Current public prices anchor buyers below viable delivery cost | High | Do not ratify or promote them until the unit model is approved |
| Pass-through revenue inflates apparent margin | Medium | Exclude pass-through from service-revenue margin reporting |
| Discounts hide weak positioning or sales qualification | Medium | Require explicit commercial rationale and realized-margin review |
| Margin target is met but acquisition cost destroys profit | Medium | Track contribution margin after offer-specific acquisition cost |
| Governance or restricted-data work is underestimated | High | Treat new data categories and controls as scoped complexity units |
| Performance pricing creates attribution disputes | High | Hold until baseline, audit, legal, and downside controls exist |
