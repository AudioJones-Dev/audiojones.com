---

## 1. Objective

Determine which identity language belongs in:

1. **search acquisition** — words the market actually uses in queries,
2. **conversion copy** — words prospects recognize when they land,
3. **brand/IP** — proprietary Audio Jones category language,
4. **professional identity** — language that accurately describes Audio Jones even when customers do not search it.

The central question is not whether a term is technically accurate. It is whether the target customer uses it naturally and whether it maps to commercially relevant search behavior.

---

## 2. Evidence States

- **KNOWN:** current AudioJones.com/repo usage.
- **RETRIEVED:** observed competitor/site language.
- **INFERRED:** plausible based on context; requires query/customer validation.
- **UNKNOWN:** no quantitative evidence yet.

No search-volume claims are made in this document.

## 5. Role Separation — Working Model

### Search acquisition language

Prefer terms that describe what the customer calls themselves, their business, or their problem:

`service business` · `business owner` · `contractor` · `home service business` · specific trade + problem

### Conversion language

Translate the search into recognized business pain:

`missed calls` · `slow follow-up` · `manual work` · `leads going cold` · `not knowing which marketing works` · `disconnected tools`

### Proprietary Audio Jones language

Retain where it creates differentiation after comprehension:

`Founder Intelligence Systems` · `ResponseOS` · `M.A.P.` · `Signal vs Noise` · `Revenue Leak Diagnostic` · `Founder Gravity Audit`

### Professional capability language

Use where it accurately explains Audio Jones's role, even if it is not a high-volume search term:

`Operations & Business Systems Engineer` · `Forward-Deployed AI & Automation Engineer`

**Working rule:** professional identity and proprietary IP do not have to be the primary SEO query vocabulary.

## 6. Local vs Remote Language Architecture — Hypothesis

The site needs to communicate two truths without creating duplicate/thin pages:

1. **Local acquisition:** Audio Jones can serve Miami / South Florida businesses and meet/operate locally.
2. **Remote scalability:** systems, AI/automation, SEO/AEO, diagnostics, and implementation can serve qualified businesses outside the local market.

A useful competitor pattern was retrieved from HeyTony's Services page:

- title: `Digital Marketing Services in Hamilton`
- description: explicitly states they are based in Hamilton while also working with clients globally.

This demonstrates one viable architecture: **strong local entity anchoring without implying local-only delivery**.

It is evidence of a pattern, not proof that the same wording is optimal for Audio Jones.

---

## 7. Falsification Questions

Before changing `founder` language, test competing explanations:

1. The ICP may not search `founder`, but founder language could still convert better after acquisition.
2. `business owner` may be more natural but too broad to attract the intended economic segment.
3. `contractor` may improve trade relevance but unintentionally exclude other service businesses.
4. `home services` may have strong demand but over-narrow Audio Jones's broader operations/AI capability.
5. Geographic terms may help local SEO but weaken remote perception if not architected carefully.
6. Proprietary language may have low existing search demand but still be valuable for branded/entity/category creation.

---

## 8. Evidence Needed to Promote or Reject Terms

For each identity term, collect:

- DataForSEO volume/related terms/SERP intent,
- GSC impressions/clicks where the site already surfaces,
- GBP search terms,
- QuestionFinder questions,
- competitor titles/H1s/page architecture,
- prospect/client transcripts and inquiry language,
- conversion performance once tested.

### Decision rule

A term becomes a canonical acquisition term only when it demonstrates a useful combination of:

`market usage × commercial intent × ICP fit × right-to-win × conversion clarity`

Search volume alone is insufficient.
