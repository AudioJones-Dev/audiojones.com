## 9. Evidence-level conclusions

1. **ResponseOS has a validated commodity bridge.** `AI receptionist` combines 12,100 volume, $33.64 CPC, commercial product SERPs, AI Overview/PAA presence, and contractor-specific competitors.
2. **The contractor modifier is commercially coherent despite low exact volume.** `AI receptionist for contractors` returned 10 volume and $40.76 CPC; its SERP is explicitly contractor-oriented.
3. **Mature receptionist/answering categories are expensive and competitive.** `virtual receptionist` and `answering service` each returned 3,600 volume with $113.63 and $183.84 CPC. Audio Jones must differentiate ResponseOS rather than claim the generic category outright.
4. **Contractor SEO is a plausible acquisition cluster.** `SEO for contractors` returned 590 volume / $14.52 CPC; `SEO for home services` returned 210 / $15.93; both SERPs contain dedicated agency/service pages.
5. **Miami local demand exists but does not justify a city page yet.** Two terms returned volume and mobile SERPs showed local packs, but Audio Jones lacks first-party GSC/GBP evidence and demonstrated local-pack right-to-win.
6. **`business systems consultant` is not a primary acquisition category.** Its 210-volume SERP is dominated by jobs and career content.
7. **Tool intent can matter without returned volume.** `missed call revenue calculator` returned no numeric metric but a stable calculator SERP; `marketing ROI calculator` returned 320 volume / $18.35 CPC.
8. **No query is ready for `PROCEED`.** GSC/GBP/customer-language evidence and a separately approved implementation experiment remain outstanding.
